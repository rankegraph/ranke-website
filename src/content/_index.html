Every release, with its evidence, kept.

A year later someone asks why you shipped with that CVE. The build logs have expired, the ticket is gone, the branch was squashed. Ranke keeps the whole record — the snapshot, the scan, the decision that was taken — and every answer points back at the document it came from.

What an archive keeps

An archive is a collection of records: letters, photographs, minutes, recordings. Each one was taken in, and the taking-in was itself recorded.

So every record carries two dates: when it entered the archive and its presumed date of origin. It carries who put it there. It carries what it is. None of this describes the world the record speaks about; it describes the record, and that is what makes the record usable as evidence rather than as information.

Nothing changes after entering the archive. A document that turns out to be wrong keeps its place; a later record says so, and both stay readable. Contradiction is part of what the archive holds, because which account is right is often decided long after both were filed.

Every record belongs to a body of material. Cataloguing, summaries and analyses are filed too, each saying what it drew on. A century of that work leaves a structure, and the structure is what makes anything findable.

Archives have done this since the nineteenth century.

Digital systems keep history: git every version of a file, an issue tracker every comment, a build system its logs. Each keeps its own, under its own policy: one squashes, one prunes after ninety days, one is append-only, and several cannot be exported at all. The history is scattered, and no system holds what runs across them.

What Ranke does with it

Ranke-Graph takes the archival form literally. Everything enters as a claim: a file, a commit, a scan result, a message. A claim holds the bytes as they were, the time it entered, who put it there, and edges to the claims it was built from. Nothing is overwritten or removed, and no claim can be written without saying what it rests on.

That constraint is where the order comes from. The structure is the citations themselves, so there is no catalogue to maintain, and following them always ends at something that entered from outside. Whatever an agent or a colleague answers from the archive, the path back to the original record is part of the answer.

Ranke-Graph is a format. The graph is the content, and any store that holds bytes under a key can hold it: a filesystem, S3, Postgres, Neo4j, or several at once. RankeDB serves it and decides who may read and write. Without RankeDB the archive is still a set of self-describing files.
